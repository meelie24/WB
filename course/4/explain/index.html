04

Understand the risk settings

Learn how categories, indicator direction and different levels of weighting fit together.

Learning stage 1 of 4
1Explain2Show me3Do it with me4Try it myself

Words you’ll need

Risk category. A group of related inherent-exposure indicators, such as Client risk.

01

Group questions by what they measure

The predefined inherent groups are nature/size/complexity, client risk, products/services and transactions, delivery channels, and geographical risk. Authorities can adapt categories to their methodology. Assign each question to the relevant category, controls, or None for general information. After distribution, categories cannot be added and questions cannot be reassigned to a different category.

02

Learn direction separately from importance

Positive correlation means higher values indicate higher risk; negative means higher values indicate lower risk. Correlation describes an inherent indicator’s direction. It is not a negative weight, a control discount or an instruction to subtract safeguards.

03

Allocate two separate percentage budgets

First divide 100% between inherent-risk categories; controls must be 0%. Then divide another 100% between questions inside each category. The tool initially uses equal weights, which the supervisor reviews against sector priorities and national methodology. Do not mix a question’s within-category share with its category’s share.

04

Response options can have weights too

Where the question format supports it, response options may use weights or exact figures. This is distinct from allocating category and question shares. Some formats do not allow particular answer-level ratings in the platform and need external Excel configuration. The predefined country list does not automatically differentiate jurisdiction risk.

05

Know what changing a weight does

Weights may be changed before or after responses. Afterwards, Calculate risk score refreshes results using the changed weights. The guide does not provide the complete scoring formula or thresholds. This course explains the settings and stale/recalculated state without inventing an official risk algorithm.
