Background and Rationale · pp. 2

Supervising Designated Non-Financial Businesses and Professions (DNFBPs) remains one of the most significant challenges in the implementation of effective AML/CFT regimes. Across numerous countries, the number of DNFBPs such as real estate agents, lawyers, accountants, trust and company service providers, and casinos has grown substantially, creating a supervisory burden that many authorities are ill-equipped to manage. Reports from the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) and various FATF-Style Regional Bodies (FSRBs) consistently point to the lack of basic identification, outreach capability, and risk understanding within supervisory authorities. This platform is being proposed to support supervisors in meeting their obligations more efficiently and in line with international standards.

Under the FATF Recommendations, DNFBPs are subject to core AML/CFT obligations covering customer due diligence, record-keeping, suspicious transaction reporting, and internal controls, broadly mirroring the requirements applicable to financial institutions. Supervisory authorities, whether government agencies or self-regulatory organizations, are expected to oversee compliance with these standards on a risk-sensitive basis and provide appropriate guidance to regulated entities.

Objective of the Tool · pp. 3

The primary objective of this tool is to assist national supervisory authorities in collecting and analysing data from DNFBPs for AML/CFT supervision purposes. The tool will enable a structured, technology-driven approach to obtaining risk-relevant information from the private sector. The tool will already include a minimum set of built-in questionnaires for each DNFBP sector, which supervisory authorities can further modify and/or expand to reflect their national context. The tool will support authorities in adopting a risk-based supervisory model, as recommended by the FATF, by allowing them to identify, assess, and mitigate inherent risks based on timely and accurate information gathered directly from the entities under their oversight. It is important to note that the tool is designed to assess inherent risk only, not residual risk, as AML/CFT mitigating factors do not contribute to the risk scoring.

Data Privacy and Ownership · pp. 3

A fundamental principle underpinning the development of this tool is the full control and ownership of data by the respective national authorities. The World Bank will not collect, store, or have access to any of the data submitted through the platform. All information will remain within the custody of the country that is using the tool. The World Bank’s role is limited to the development and technical customization of the tool.

This stage focuses on developing the actual content of the questionnaire. Use the Questionnaire Builder to design surveys tailored to the specific needs and risks of each sector. A well-structured questionnaire ensures you capture the right data in a clear, logical sequence. Follow the steps outlines in this guide below to create an effective and targeted questionnaire.

Define your Questionnaire

Start by creating a new questionnaire (see, Figure 7).

Name: Give it a clear, descriptive title so it is easy to identify later (e.g., “Real Estate Sector – Pilot Survey”).

Description: Explain the purpose of the questionnaire — this helps others understand its scope and intent at a glance.

Sector: Assign the correct sector tag to make analysis and reporting more relevant.

Pages: Start adding pages to the questionnaire.

Note: You can only ‘Add Page’ after you fill provide information under ‘Name’, ‘Description’ and ‘Sector’; otherwise, this icon will not be activated.

Figure 7: Creating New Questionnaire

Important: Defining these basics upfront keeps your forms organized, searchable, and aligned with their intended use.

Structure the Questionnaire with Steps

Break the questionnaire into logical steps (like Entity Identification, General Information, Customers, Transactions etc.).

Provide clear step titles to guide respondents through the process (e.g., Entity Identification, General Information, etc.). These serve as section headings used to organize the questionnaire.

Always begin with a start page as your first step. From there, set the type field according to the step's position in the questionnaire: the first step must be set to start type, all intermediate steps to page type, and the last step to end type. This structure helps organize the flow of the questionnaire.

Before you can add any questions to a step, you must complete three required fields: title, next page, and type. Leaving any of these blank will prevent you from proceeding.

Use the next page field to define the order in which respondents move through the steps, this controls their journey from one section to the next. If a step does not lead anywhere, selecting None is acceptable; however, the field must not be left blank, as this will block you from moving forward.

Add conditional steps if you need to show or hide parts based on earlier answers.

Note: You must first complete the section details under Steps. This includes entering the Title, selecting the Next Page, and choosing the Type. Once these fields have been completed, you can then proceed to add the questions and any conditions for that section.

Figure 8: Structuring Steps

Note: Steps make long forms more manageable and allow you to group related questions together. Conditional routing personalizes the experience and collects only relevant data.

Important: Use the “Add Conditionals” feature to control which steps or questions respondents see based on their previous answers. This allows the questionnaire to adapt dynamically to each respondent’s situation, making the survey more relevant, reducing unnecessary questions, and improving overall data quality.

If the respondent answers “No,” the follow-up page can be skipped entirely.

Conditionalities are particularly useful for:

skipping sections that do not apply to certain respondents;

Carefully designing conditional paths helps ensure respondents only see questions that are applicable to them, resulting in cleaner and more accurate data collection.

Add Questions Thoughtfully

Within each step, define the questions that fit that section’s focus.

Pick the question type that best matches the information you need (e.g., Single Choice for Yes/No, Long Text for explanations).

Write clear, specific question text (e.g., what was the total annual turnover of the entity in the Financial Year 2023-24?).

Use descriptions to clarify what’s expected, if needed.

Mark questions as required only when absolutely necessary.

For choice questions, define clear, non-overlapping options.

Each question must be associated with a risk category—such as nature, size and complexity of the business, client risk, product/services and transaction risk, delivery channel risk, geographical risk — or AML/CFT controls. If no specific category applies, select ‘None’ from the drop-down menu. In this case, the question will be treated as general information.

Correlation: When adding a risk category, you may choose to keep the “Positive Correlation” option selected or uncheck it to apply a negative correlation. For clarity purposes, a positive correlation means that a higher number/values corresponds to a higher risk, whereas a negative correlation means that a higher number/values corresponds to lower risk. The appropriate selection will depend on the nature of the question and how its responses relate to risk.

It is important to note that selecting a negative correlation does not reduce or offset risk and should not be interpreted as mitigating factor. In all cases, the indicator reflects inherent risk and will contribute to the overall score. The distinction lies only in the direction of the relationship – whether higher or lower values indicate greater risk – not its impact on the final risk score.

Click add Option to take multiple responses on the question. The tool also allows to capture an ‘Exact Figure’ when required. Additionally, you can assign weights for each option (e.g., 1, 2, 3 etc.), depending on the level of risk or important you decide to attribute to each response.

Preview Questionnaire

Always preview the entire flow before publishing:

Check that steps follow the intended sequence.

Verify that conditionals work as expected.

Make sure mandatory fields and validations are correct.

When you are confident everything works, save your questionnaire.

This pilot testing helps identify any gaps in logic or usability issues in advance, preventing confusion and reducing the need for revisions later.

Stage 6: Risk Scoring and Adjusting Weights · pp. 21

When creating the questionnaire, use “Adjust Weight” to review the questions assigned to each risk category. The tool automatically distributes weights across all risk categories so that they collectively total 100%. Likewise, questions within each category are initially assigned equal weights that also add up to 100%.

The tool is intended to assess inherent risk only. For this reason, if “AML/CFT controls” are included as a category, it is recommended to assign it a weight of 0%. The system does not apply adjustments based on the strength or effectiveness of controls and therefore does not calculate residual risk.

Adjusting Risk Category Weights: Supervisory authorities can adjust the weight of each risk category to better reflect their supervisory priorities and understanding of the sector. For example, all categories may be weighted equally, or some categories may be assigned a higher percentage where they are considered more relevant or higher risk. Regardless of the approach used, the total weight across all inherent risk categories must always equal 100%.

Adjusting question weights: The same approach applies to individual questions within each category. Supervisors may increase or reduce the weight of certain questions depending on how important they are considered for the overall risk assessment. This allows the questionnaire to better capture the specific risks and characteristics of the supervised sector.

Flexibility to update the scoring: Weights are not fixed permanently and can be reviewed or adjusted at any stage of the process — both before the questionnaire is sent and after responses have been received. This gives supervisory authorities flexibility to refine the methodology over time and adapt the scoring to changing risks, supervisory experience, or emerging priorities.

8.1 View surveys

View surveys : Allows to review individual responses submitted by each entity, analyse answers to specific questions, and compare responses across entities.

Once a particular survey has been selected, you can visualize the name of the reporting entity, if the survey was completed or still pending. If the survey is completed, the tool will allow you to revise the answers (questionnaire mode), see the analysis and deleted, if needed (eg. case of duplication).

8.1.a Answer analysis

This feature allows supervisors to analyze the risk scoring of an individual institution in comparison with the rest of the respondent population.

For each question, the system identifies the lowest and highest values submitted across all responses. It then places the individual institution’s response within that range and assesses how it compares relative to other respondents. In this way, the scoring is not based only on the institution’s standalone answer, but also on how that answer compares to the overall population of supervised entities.

This functionality helps supervisors better identify institutions that may present comparatively higher or lower risk exposures for specific questions or indicators. Supervisors can use the option on the top of this page, as a paper version of this analysis can be useful during onsite inspections.

8.2 Download

Download : Allows supervisors to export all responses into an Excel file for further analysis. The file is downloaded directly to the user’s computer and can be used to review the data in more detail.

While the tool provides structured data collection and an initial risk analysis, the Excel export gives supervisory authorities greater flexibility to explore the information further and perform their own customized analysis. For example, supervisors may wish to identify institutions with exposure to certain high-risk jurisdictions, products, or customer categories. The system may not automatically highlight these aspects where no specific weighting has been assigned, but the exported data allows supervisors to examine them separately.

Supervisory authorities are encouraged to use this functionality to carry out more detailed analysis of individual responses and questions. In practice, the Aggregate Analysis feature is designed to provide a higher-level view of overall institutional risk classification, while the Excel export supports deeper review and more targeted supervisory analysis based on the authority’s own priorities and areas of focus.

8.3 Calculate risk score

Calculate risk score : Recalculates risk scores based on the assigned weights. It is important to note that if you change weights of any risk categories or individual questions after receiving responses, you must “Calculate risk scores” to update the results. Without this step, the analysis will not reflect the revised scoring.

8.4 Aggregate analysis

Aggregate analysis : Provides a visual overview of how institutions are distributed across the overall risk spectrum. The Fragmentation setting allows supervisors to control how detailed the visualization becomes. A lower fragmentation level (e.g., 5) groups institutions into broader categories, making it easier to obtain a quick, high-level picture of the sector. A higher fragmentation level (e.g., 50 or 100) breaks the results into smaller intervals, helping supervisors identify patterns, concentrations, and more subtle differences between institutions’ overall risk scores. This can support more targeted monitoring planning and resource allocation.

The colours in the chart also help interpret the results at a glance. Green represents lower overall risk, while colours moving toward orange and red indicate increasing levels of risk. In the example shown, no institution appears in dark red because none reached the highest aggregated risk category calculated by the system. This does not necessarily mean that no institution presents higher risks in certain areas. For example, some institutions may still show elevated risks in specific products, customer types, geographic exposure, or individual assessment questions. The aggregate analysis reflects the institution’s overall calculated score after all risk factors are combined and weighted together.

Before sending the surveys out to the reporting entities:

Test internally to confirm questions are accurate, risk categories are included, and errors are eliminated.

Notify the sector ahead of time. The tool can't send deadline or consequence reminders, so reporting entities should be informed early about required data.

Be aware that your official email may end up in recipients' spam folders. It's advisable to highlight this on your official website when announcing the launch of the data collection process.

Clearly instruct supervised entities to ‘PRINT’ the questionnaire before clicking ‘COMPLETE’ button, as clicking ‘complete’ button will submit the questionnaire to the supervised authority and prevent them from printing their responses afterwards.

Other considerations

The tool provides default risk scores for various categories and individual questions. However, supervisors should review these settings and adjust them to suit their own country context and priorities. This process includes reconsidering the weight of each risk category ensuring individual questions are matched with the most appropriate category.

Whenever a survey is distributed, a version of it is generated. If the survey is sent out multiple times without altering its questions or risk categories, those responses will be grouped under the same version. On the other hand, even a single change in a question creates a new survey version, meaning responses are collected separately, and the aggregated data is isolated for that specific variation.
